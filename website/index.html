Virtual Volume

Your volume button, rebuilt for your screen.

A thin control lives on the edge of your display. Tap it, swipe it up, swipe it down, or drag for a smooth sweep — over any app, without opening anything. It drives the real hardware volume through the standard Android audio APIs.

How it works

Three steps, then forget about it

Setup takes under a minute and the app walks you through every step on first launch.

  1. 1

    Allow the overlay

    Android calls it “Display over other apps”. It is the only permission Virtual Volume needs to draw the control above your current app.

  2. 2

    Enable the control

    A lightweight foreground service keeps the control available. Android requires a quiet notification for that, and it doubles as your off switch.

  3. 3

    Use the edge

    The line sits on the left or right edge, wherever you put it. Tap the top half to raise, the bottom half to lower, or drag to sweep.

Features

Built like the button it replaces

Subtle until you touch it

Idle opacity, active opacity, length and thickness are all yours. The default is translucent — never invisible, never in the way.

Stays put when you rotate

Position is stored relative to the current screen, not as raw coordinates, so the control keeps its edge and its spot in portrait and both landscape orientations.

Only as big as it needs to be

The touch window is a narrow strip on the edge — there is no invisible sheet covering your screen, so the rest of the app underneath stays fully usable.

Real volume, real limits

Every change goes through AudioManager and is clamped to the device's actual range. Nothing is simulated and nothing is cached.

Quick Settings tile

Add the tile once and switch the control on or off straight from the notification shade, in perfect sync with the app.

Designed against accidents

A touch-slop dead zone, a per-event step cap and reversal re-anchoring mean a resting thumb or a pocket will not move your volume.

Optional haptics

One short tick per level, like the detent on a physical rocker. Turn it off, or keep it — it never fires more than it should.

Dark-first, light optional

A calm dark interface with an optional light theme, Material 3 typography and accessible contrast throughout.

Media today, more later

Media volume is the default because it is what you reach for most. Ring, alarm, notification, call and system streams are already modelled.

Compatibility

What Android allows, stated plainly

Virtual Volume implements the maximum Android officially permits — and says so when the platform draws a line.

Works

  • Android 8.0 (API 26) and newer
  • Over ordinary apps, games and browsers
  • Portrait, landscape and both landscape rotations
  • Restores itself after a reboot when you allow it
  • Phones and tablets, any screen size or density

Restricted by Android

  • Screen off. Touch events are not delivered to overlays while the display is powered down. No app can change this, and Virtual Volume does not pretend otherwise.
  • Lock screen and secure surfaces. Payment sheets and some system dialogs hide every overlay by design.
  • Background starts on Android 12+. Occasionally the system refuses a restart after boot; opening the app or tapping the tile brings it straight back.
  • OEM battery rules. Some skins need an exemption from battery optimisation.

Quick Settings

One tap from the shade

Add the Virtual Volume tile once and the control is a swipe away. The tile reads the same state as the app, so the two can never disagree.

  1. Swipe down twice to open the full Quick Settings panel.
  2. Tap the pencil (Edit) icon.
  3. Drag Virtual Volume into your active tiles.

Android does not let an app add a tile to your panel by itself — that step is always yours.

FAQ

Questions worth answering

Does it work while the screen is off?

No, and no Android app can. When the display is powered down the system stops delivering touch events to overlays. Virtual Volume keeps its service and your settings alive, and the control reappears — correctly positioned, even if you rotated while the screen was off — the moment the display turns back on.

Why does it need the “Display over other apps” permission?

Because the whole point is that the control is reachable while you are inside another app. Android gates that behind SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW. You can revoke it at any time from system settings; the app detects that and tells you instead of failing silently.

Why is there a permanent notification?

Android requires a visible notification for any foreground service — it is how the system tells you something is running in the background. The notification is silent, has no sound or badge, and its “Turn off” action stops the control instantly.

Will it drain my battery?

The overlay is a small, hardware-accelerated view that draws only when it changes. There is no polling loop, no wake lock and no background work while you are not touching it.

Can it change ringer or alarm volume?

Media volume is the default. Ring, alarm, notification, call and system streams are modelled in the app already and can be switched on in the Audio section.

Is it on Google Play?

The APK is published directly from this repository's GitHub Releases. Every build is produced by CI from the published source and signed with the release key, so what you download is reproducible from the code you can read.

How do I move or resize the control?

Open the app and use the Control section: edge, position, length, thickness, idle opacity, active opacity, touch-area size and swipe sensitivity all update the live control immediately — and the in-app preview is the real control, not a mock-up.

What if my phone's OEM blocks it?

Some skins add their own rules on top of Android. Exclude Virtual Volume from battery optimisation and make sure “Display over other apps” is allowed. If a start is refused, the app surfaces the system's reason on the dashboard rather than hiding it.
